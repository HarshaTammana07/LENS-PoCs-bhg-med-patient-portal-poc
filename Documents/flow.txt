This matches the BHG operating model very closely. In the source, the formal patient journey is described as intake / PPA → medical exam + counselor + consents → first dose → daily treatment loop → reassessment → discharge, transfer, or restart.

The most important thing to understand is that there are really three journeys happening in parallel.

1. Access journey
Need Help
→ Find BHG
→ Contact / Referral
→ PPA
→ Appointment
→ Eligibility
→ Insurance / Payment Setup
→ Intake

This determines whether the patient can actually enter treatment. BHG's source specifically connects this area to appointments, Salesforce IDs, referral sources, eligibility, payer assignment, prior authorization, hardship, and self-pay setup.

2. Clinical journey
Clinical Intake
→ Enrollment
→ Treatment Plan
→ Medication Order
→ Dose
→ Counseling
→ UA / Labs
→ Reassessment
→ Continued Treatment
→ Discharge / Transfer

Once enrolled, the patient becomes part of the census. From there, the recurring BHG experience is primarily check-in → dosing → counseling when required → UA/labs → reassessment.

3. Financial journey

This is happening behind the clinical journey:

Insurance Card / Self-Pay
        ↓
Eligibility Verification
        ↓
Payer Assignment
        ↓
Prior Authorization
        ↓
Treatment Delivered
        ↓
Bill / Insurance Claim
        ↓
Paid / Denied
        ↓
AR Follow-Up / Rebill

For patients without sufficient coverage, there can also be:

Payment Issue
→ Hardship / FMP
→ Continue Treatment where approved

That part matters because BHG is not simply providing clinical treatment; it also has to make sure the treatment is reimbursable and the patient can remain in care.
